# Juniper Market Accounts Payable Policy

Juniper Market, 410 Alder Street, Bend, Oregon. Version 4, in force from October 1, 2026. This is a fictional document written for a lab: Juniper Market, its people and its suppliers do not exist.

## 1. Purpose

This policy says how Juniper Market pays its suppliers. It exists for three reasons. We pay what we owe, on time, so suppliers keep delivering. We never pay for goods we did not order or did not receive. And every payment can be explained later, by anyone, from the records alone. The policy applies to every invoice for goods and services, whoever handles it: a clerk, the accounts payable manager, or the payables agent. When a case is not covered here, the invoice is put on hold and the accounts payable manager decides.

## 2. Who does what

Purchasing writes purchase orders and agrees prices with suppliers. Receiving counts what arrives at the dock and records it the same day. Accounts payable checks invoices and prepares payments. The accounts payable manager, Dana Whitaker, approves payments. The store director approves the large ones (section 13). The payables agent is software that works for accounts payable: it may read invoices and purchase orders, put an invoice on hold, and prepare a payment. It may not approve a payment, change a purchase order, or change supplier records.

## 3. The Friday payment run

Juniper Market pays suppliers once a week, on Friday. The run covers every open invoice that is due on that Friday. Invoices are checked on Thursday. The list of payments is ready for approval by Thursday at 3:00 PM Pacific time, and approved payments are sent to the bank on Friday morning. An invoice that is not approved by then waits for the next run. Nobody starts a second run in the same week without the approval of the accounts payable manager, because two runs are how the same invoice gets paid twice.

## 4. The three-way match

An invoice is paid only when three records agree: the purchase order (what we ordered and at what price), the receipt (what arrived) and the invoice (what the supplier bills). Each line of the invoice is compared with the line of the purchase order that has the same product code. The quantity billed may not be higher than the quantity ordered or the quantity received. The unit price must be the price on the purchase order. When all lines agree, the invoice matches and can be scheduled for payment.

## 5. Quantity differences

When an invoice bills more units than the purchase order shows, the invoice is put on hold. We do not pay the higher amount and we do not correct the invoice ourselves. The hold reason names the product, the quantity billed, the quantity ordered and the amount of the difference in dollars. Purchasing then asks the supplier for a corrected invoice or a credit memo. When an invoice bills fewer units than ordered, it is paid as billed, because the supplier may bill the rest later.

## 6. Price differences

The unit price on the invoice must equal the unit price on the purchase order. Any difference, even a few cents, puts the invoice on hold. The hold reason names the product, both prices and the dollar difference for the whole line. Only purchasing may accept a new price, and it does so by changing the purchase order, never by a note on the invoice. A price list from the supplier is not an agreement. The price that counts is the one on the purchase order on the day the order was accepted.

## 7. Invoices without a purchase order

No payment without a purchase order. An invoice that names no purchase order, or names one we cannot find, is put on hold. There is one exception: an invoice under $250 may be paid when the accounts payable manager approves it in writing and names the budget it belongs to. An invoice of $250 or more with no purchase order is never paid as it is. It stays on hold until purchasing issues a purchase order after the fact and signs for it, or the invoice goes back to the supplier.

## 8. Partial deliveries

Sometimes fewer units arrive than the supplier bills, because part of the order is late or was refused at the dock. In that case pay only for the units received, at the purchase order price. The payment is scheduled for the lower amount, and the payment note says how many units arrived, how many were billed, and the dollar amount held back. The rest is paid when receiving records the remaining units, or it is cleared by a credit memo. Only receiving can say what arrived. A delivery slip sent by the supplier is not proof.

## 9. Duplicate invoices

Before an invoice is paid, check whether the same supplier has already billed the same purchase order for the same amount. If it has, the later invoice is a duplicate, whatever its number, and it is put on hold with the number of the earlier invoice in the reason. Suppliers send duplicates by mistake when a first invoice was mailed and then emailed, or when their system reissues an invoice. A duplicate is never paid and never deleted. It stays on hold until the supplier confirms in writing that it can be voided.

## 10. Freight and delivery charges

Freight is paid only when it is on the purchase order or covered by a written agreement with that supplier. A freight line that is on neither puts the invoice on hold. Agreements about freight are made by purchasing and are kept with the supplier's records. They name the supplier, say how freight is billed and give the highest amount that may be paid without asking. Fuel surcharges, pallet fees and liftgate fees follow the same rule as freight. A charge above the agreed amount is held, even when the rest of the invoice matches.

## 11. Sales tax

Goods that Juniper Market buys to sell again are not taxed, and our resale certificate is on file with every supplier of goods. An invoice for resale goods that adds sales tax is put on hold, and the supplier is asked for a corrected invoice. Supplies we use ourselves, such as cleaning products and office paper, may carry tax where the supplier's state requires it. Oregon has no state sales tax, so a tax line from an Oregon supplier is always checked by a person before payment.

## 12. Payment terms and discounts

Each supplier has payment terms, such as Net 15, Net 21 or Net 30. The term starts on the invoice date. An invoice is paid in the Friday run on or just before its due date, not earlier, so cash stays in the business. When a supplier offers a discount for early payment, for example 2 percent within 10 days, accounts payable takes it only when the invoice already matches. We never pay an invoice that does not match in order to catch a discount.

## 13. Approval limits

Every payment needs the approval of a person. The accounts payable manager approves payments up to $5,000. A payment above $5,000 also needs the store director. A payment above $25,000 needs the owner. The limits apply to one invoice, and splitting an invoice to stay under a limit is not allowed. The person who approves checks the supplier, the invoice number, the amount and the note. They may approve the payment, change the amount and give a reason, or reject it. The payables agent prepares payments and never approves one.

## 14. How we pay

Payments go out by bank transfer on Friday morning. We do not pay by paper check, cash, gift card or payment app. A wire transfer on any other day needs two approvals, from the accounts payable manager and the store director, and a reason in writing. Each payment carries a note with the supplier's name, the invoice number and the purchase order number, so the supplier can match it. One payment pays one invoice. We do not combine invoices into one payment, because a combined payment is hard to trace.

## 15. Changes to a supplier's bank details

Bank details are changed only by the accounts payable manager, and never because of an email, a letter or a note on an invoice. When a supplier asks for a change, the manager calls the supplier on the phone number already in our records, not a number given in the request, and confirms the change with a person there. The change waits one full payment run before it is used. Requests to pay a new account in a hurry are a common kind of fraud, and urgency is a reason to slow down.

## 16. New suppliers

A new supplier is paid only after purchasing has set it up: a signed W-9 form, an address, a phone number, payment terms and bank details confirmed by phone. An invoice from a company that is not in our supplier records is put on hold, even when the amount is small and the goods are in the store. Accounts payable does not create suppliers, and the payables agent cannot create or change a supplier at all.

## 17. Credit memos and returns

When goods go back to a supplier, or an invoice was too high, the supplier sends a credit memo. A credit memo is matched to its invoice and purchase order like an invoice. It reduces the next payment to that supplier, and the payment note names both documents. We do not reduce a payment because someone promised a credit on the phone. Until the credit memo arrives, the invoice it belongs to stays on hold or is short-paid under section 8.

## 18. Holds

A hold stops an invoice from being paid, and it always has a reason. The reason is written so that a person who has never seen the invoice understands it: what is wrong, on which line, and by how many dollars. A hold is released only by the accounts payable manager, after the cause is fixed: a corrected invoice, a credit memo, a changed purchase order or a new receipt. An invoice on hold is looked at again every Thursday. An invoice that has been on hold for 30 days goes back to the supplier with a letter.

## 19. Disputes with a supplier

Purchasing talks to suppliers about prices, quantities and quality. Accounts payable talks to suppliers about payment dates and remittance. The payables agent does not contact suppliers. When a supplier calls about an unpaid invoice, the person who answers looks up the hold reason and reads it to the supplier. We do not promise a payment date for an invoice on hold. If the supplier threatens to stop deliveries, the store director is told the same day.

## 20. Late fees and interest

We pay on time, so late fees should be rare. A late fee on an invoice is paid only when the delay was ours and the supplier agreement allows the fee. When an invoice was late because it was on hold for a good reason, such as a wrong quantity, the fee is not paid, and the hold reason is sent to the supplier. A late fee is never added to the payment of another invoice. It is billed and matched on its own.

## 21. Small differences and rounding

Totals are checked to the cent. A difference in the invoice total of less than $1.00 that comes from rounding a line, with every quantity and unit price correct, may be paid, and the payment note says so. This rule covers rounding only. It does not cover a different unit price, a different quantity or an extra line, however small. When in doubt, hold the invoice. A held invoice costs a phone call, and a wrong payment costs more.

## 22. Records

Every invoice, purchase order, receipt, credit memo, hold and payment is kept for seven years. Records are kept in the business database, not in email. A paper invoice is scanned on the day it arrives, and its text is stored with the invoice. Nobody edits a stored invoice. A correction is a new document from the supplier. The log of who read or changed a record is also kept for seven years, for people and for software alike.

## 23. Separation of duties

No one person can both create a cost and pay it. The person who writes a purchase order does not approve its payment. The person who counts a delivery does not check its invoice. The person who approves a payment did not prepare it. The same rule applies to software: the payables agent prepares payments, a person approves them, and the two are never the same account. Shared passwords are not allowed, and each person and each program has its own login.

## 24. The payables agent

The payables agent is a program that checks invoices and prepares the Friday run. It follows this policy like any clerk. It reads data only through the tools it was given. It states facts it has read and does not guess a quantity, a price or a date. For every invoice it either prepares a payment or puts the invoice on hold with a reason, and it names the section of this policy when a rule decides the case. Every payment it prepares waits for a person. When it is unsure, it holds the invoice and says why.

## 25. Exceptions

An exception to this policy is allowed only in writing, from the accounts payable manager, for one invoice at a time. The exception says which rule is set aside, why, and for how much. Exceptions above $5,000 also need the store director. A rule that needs an exception every week is a rule to change, and the manager brings it to the yearly review. Nobody, and no program, may treat an old exception as a standing permission.
